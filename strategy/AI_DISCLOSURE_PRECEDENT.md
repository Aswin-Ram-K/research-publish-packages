## 0. The one-paragraph answer

The field has **a norm for papers that used LLMs as part of the method, and almost no norm for disclosing LLM help with the *writing*.** Where venues speak, they agree on three things and disagree on nothing important: the human author is fully responsible, the AI is never an author, and any *significant* use should be documented. What they disagree about is **where** it goes and **whether it is mandatory**: ICLR 2026 now requires a separate LLM-usage section and threatens desk rejection; ICML merely "encourages"; TMLR does not ask for disclosure at all; Science wants the full prompt, tool and version, in the cover letter, acknowledgements *and* methods. AI labs themselves are **largely silent** about their own LLM use — I grepped ten of their flagship public reports and found no self-disclosure sentence (method caveat in §2). There is therefore no "AI lab pattern" to copy, and the honest recommendation is to disclose at the strictest plausible reading of your target venue, in one short factual paragraph.

**What the field does NOT have a norm for** (stated explicitly, as required):
1. **Disclosing LLM assistance with prose as a mandatory item** at NeurIPS, ICML or TMLR. ICLR 2026 is the exception; ACL/ARR is the other.
2. **Naming the model and version.** Only Science's methods rule demands the tool and its version; I found no other venue text requiring a version string.
3. **A negative statement** ("no AI was used"). I searched for real ones and found none (§3, gap).
4. **Lab-side self-disclosure.** No lab-policy or paper statement found (§2).
5. **A standard location.** The same disclosure legimately belongs in an acknowledgements section, an appendix, a method section, or a dedicated "LLM usage" section depending on the venue.

### NeurIPS (2025 policy; 2026 handbook)
- Source: https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2025/LLM
  - "We welcome authors to use any tool that is suitable for preparing high-quality papers and research."
  - "we expect authors to document their methodology clearly for upholding scientific rigorousness and transparency standards"
  - "The use of LLMs in implementing the method should be described in the experimental setup section (or equivalent) if it is an important, original, or non-standard component of the approach."
  - "The use of spell checkers and grammar suggestions, programming aid for editing purposes does not need to be documented."
  - "authors are responsible for the entire content of the paper, including all text, figures, and references" / "they must ensure that all content is correct and original"
- Source: https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2025/CallForPapers — "The NeurIPS policy for use of Large Language Models (LLMs) can be found here. Authors are responsible for following it." (the link resolves to the `/Conferences/2025/LLM` page above).
- **Verdict:** (a) **required** when the LLM is an important/original/non-standard part of the method; routine writing aids are explicitly exempt; (b) document the methodology, and describe LLM method use in the experimental setup; (c) the portion I fetched **does not state an authorship rule** — **NOT VERIFIED** on authorship (`https://neurips.cc/public/LLMPolicy` is a 404).
- Also fetched: NeurIPS Code of Ethics https://neurips.cc/public/EthicsGuidelines and NeurIPS 2026 Main Track Handbook https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook — a keyword scan found **no AI/LLM disclosure clause** in either.

### ICML (2025 Call for Papers)
- Source: https://icml.cc/Conferences/2025/CallForPapers
  - "Use of Generative AI: Authors are allowed to use generative AI tools such as Large Language Models (LLMs) to assist in writing or research."
  - "However, authors must take full responsibility for all content in their paper, including any content generated by AI tools that might be construed as plagiarism or scientific misconduct."
  - "We encourage authors to explain any notable ways in which these tools were used in their research methodology."
  - "LLMs are not eligible for authorship."
- The ICML 2025 Author Instructions page (https://icml.cc/Conferences/2025/AuthorInstructions) only points at the Call for Papers.
- **Verdict:** (a) **encouraged, not required**; (b) explain notable uses in the research methodology; (c) **no** — "LLMs are not eligible for authorship."

### ICLR (2026 Author Guide; 2025 guide silent)
- Source: https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuide
  - "The Use of Large Language Models (LLMs) The use of LLMs is allowed as a general-purpose assist tool."
  - "However, new this year, if LLMs played a significant role in research ideation and/or writing to the extent that they could be regarded as a contributor, then authors should describe the precise role of the LLM in a separate section on LLM usage."
  - "Not disclosing significant LLM usage can lead to desk rejection of the paper."
  - "Irrespective of the ways that LLMs were used in a given submission, authors should understand that they take full responsibility for the contents written under their name, including content generated by LLMs that could be construed as plagiarism or scientific misconduct (e.g., fabrication of facts)."
  - "LLMs are not eligible for authorship."
- **Verdict:** (a) **required** where the LLM's role was significant (research ideation and/or writing); (b) the precise role, in a **separate "LLM usage" section**; (c) **no**.
- Contrast, fetched the same way and found silent: ICLR 2025 Author Guide https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2025/AuthorGuide and the ICLR Code of Ethics https://iclr.cc/public/CodeOfEthics contain **no AI/LLM clause**.

### TMLR
- Source: https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html
  - "LLM use policy for authors LLMs may be used as general-purpose assistive tools."
  - "Whichever tools are used, authors are fully responsible for content on which they are listed as (co-) authors. This includes, but is not limited to, content generated by LLMs that could be construed as plagiarism or scientific misconduct (e.g., fabrication of facts)."
  - "Low-quality contributions (be they submissions or reviews) that appear to be largely LLM-generated will be closely examined for evidence of the issues mentioned previously, such as scientific misconduct."
  - "LLMs are not eligible for authorship."
  - "We will periodically revise this policy as new information about the use of LLMs in the scientific process becomes available."
- **Verdict:** (a) **silent on disclosure** — the policy speaks about responsibility, not about being told what you used; (b) nothing specified; (c) **no**.

## 4. "AI cannot be an author" — primary sources

- **COPE** (https://publicationethics.org/cope-position-statements/ai-author, fetched via stealth scrape): "COPE joins organisations, such as WAME and the JAMA Network among others, to state that AI tools cannot be listed as an author of a paper." and the reason: "AI tools cannot meet the requirements for authorship as they cannot take responsibility for the submitted work. As non-legal entities, they cannot assert the presence or absence of conflicts of interest nor manage copyright and license agreements."
- Venues stating it directly, all fetched:
  - ICML 2025: "LLMs are not eligible for authorship." (https://icml.cc/Conferences/2025/CallForPapers)
  - ICLR 2026: "LLMs are not eligible for authorship." (https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuide)
  - TMLR: "LLMs are not eligible for authorship." (https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
  - ACL/ARR: "Generally, generative AI tools do not qualify for authorship." (https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)
  - arXiv: "generative AI language tools should not be listed as an author" (https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)
  - Science (Wayback snapshot): "AI-assisted technologies ... do not meet the Science journals' criteria for authorship and therefore may not be listed as authors or coauthors" (https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://www.science.org/content/page/science-journals-editorial-policies)

**Why crediting it as an author would cost him, in two sentences.** An AI author cannot sign a copyright transfer or license, cannot declare competing interests, and cannot be held to account for the content, so listing one puts the venue's own terms and the paper's legal standing in breach while giving the author nothing that a disclosure does not. If a venue or reader treats a listed AI co-author as an unaccountable contributor, the human author keeps 100% of the liability and the paper carries a permanent integrity flag — whereas the *same* assistance, credited in a disclosure with a responsibility statement, is exactly what COPE, ACL, ICLR, ICML, TMLR, arXiv and Science ask for.

## 5. The recommendation

### 5.1 Recommended disclosure paragraph (single-author ML preprint)

> **Use of AI tools.** I used a large language model, *⟨tool, version, month⟩*, in two ways: to help design and implement the analysis and the experiment, and to help draft and edit this manuscript. The research idea, the experimental design, and all editorial decisions are my own. I checked and verified all AI-assisted output, and I take full responsibility for the entire content of this paper. The AI system is not an author and is not credited as a contributor.

Drop the second sentence in the version-number brackets if the venue does not ask for the tool — but keeping the tool name costs nothing and satisfies arXiv's "report significant use" and ACL's "scope and nature" in one line.

### 5.2 Minimal two-sentence variant (for venues that want the least)

> **Use of AI tools.** A large language model (*⟨tool, version⟩*) helped design and run the experiment and helped draft and edit the text; I designed the research, made every editorial decision, verified all output, and take full responsibility for the paper. The AI system is not an author.

### 5.3 Venue table

### 5.4 Honest risk

- **The analysis half of your use is the part that venues actually regulate.** COPE requires disclosure for AI used "in the collection and analysis of data"; Science for AI used "as components of their research study"; ICLR 2026 for "research ideation"; NeurIPS for LLM use "in implementing the method". Prose help is the *safer* half almost everywhere — at NeurIPS it is explicitly exempt when it is just editing.
- **Where non-disclosure is a live risk:** ICLR 2026 ("Not disclosing significant LLM usage can lead to desk rejection of the paper"), ACL/ARR (the checklist answer is mandatory and shown to reviewers, who may "flag a paper for case-by-case ethics review"), Science ("Editors may decline to move forward with manuscripts if AI is used inappropriately"). COPE's wording makes undisclosed use in data collection/analysis a responsibility breach the author is "liable for" — i.e. **yes, at least one body (COPE) treats undisclosed AI use in analysis, not just writing, as a publication-ethics matter.**
- **Where it is not:** ICML is only "encouraged", TMLR does not ask, and NeurIPS explicitly exempts routine writing aids. For an ICML/TMLR/NeurIPS submission, silence about prose help would not be a violation on the text I fetched.
- **Do not name the AI as an author or co-author** anywhere, and do not put AI-generated images in the paper at Science or in Nature's risky tier.
- **Do not overclaim compliance.** If the venue asks for a version or prompt and you do not have it, say so; inventing a version string is worse than omitting it.

## Correction (appended 2026-10-17) — TMLR is NOT silent on AI-use disclosure

The entry above records this workspace's earlier position: that TMLR's author guidance says nothing about
disclosing AI/LLM use. **That claim is wrong.** This correction supersedes it; the original text is left in
place above as history.

TMLR's own FAQ, on authors using LLM-based tools for writing or research, states: "transparency is always good,
so we also require that authors mention explicitly in their submission that they have used this tool, as a
footnote on the first page."

So TMLR does not merely tolerate disclosure — it **requires** a first-page footnote naming the tool use. Any
TMLR-bound artifact from this workspace must therefore carry that footnote on page 1, not only a back-matter
"Use of AI tools" paragraph. Done in `scratch/utmlr2/tmlr/main.tex`: an unmarked `\blfootnote` placed
immediately after `\maketitle`, verified present on page 1 of the compiled PDF (16 pages, Tectonic exit 0,
0 errors). A back-matter paragraph is retained as well, genericised to name no harness, repository or model route.
