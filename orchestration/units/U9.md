# U9 — latency contrast: is a cache hit actually faster?

**Purpose.** U2 proved the cache *meter* is aligned to the 64-token grid. That is a claim about
accounting, not about speed. This unit settles whether a prompt-cache hit reduces TTFT, so the paper
knows whether it may claim *faster* or only *fewer uncached tokens*. A null result was pre-declared a
valid, publishable answer.

**Hard bound:** ≤ 200 live requests, ≤ 30 min, 3 consecutive failures ⇒ STOP. Write paths (nothing
else): `experiments/context-cache/cells/U9/**`, `orchestration/handoffs/U9.json`,
`orchestration/units/U9.md`.

## Design (pre-registered before the first live request)

* **One variable: cache state.** Within a repetition the miss and hit prompts are **byte identical**;
  only the server's radix-cache state differs.
* **Cold is forced by a fresh 16-hex salt** at the front of message 0, so the radix prefix match is 0
  by construction (measured: `cached_tokens == 0` on every cold trial). A fresh salt per repetition.
  **`POST /flush_cache` was never called** — it would wipe the shared radix tree for every tenant.
* **Order interleaved.** Reps 0/2/4/6 are `A`: cold measured, then the identical bytes. Reps 1/3/5 are
  `B`: one unmeasured prime seeds the cache, the **hit is measured**, then a second fresh-salted
  **cold** is measured — so `B` measures the hit *before* the miss. Each rep yields exactly one paired
  `(miss, hit)` delta.
* **Strictly serial.** One request in flight at all times (`max_running_requests=5`, so any
  concurrency would corrupt TTFT). `max_tokens=1`, `temperature=0`, `thinking=False`, `stream=True`,
  `stream_options.include_usage` so TTFT and `cached_tokens` come from **one** request.
* **Lengths calibrated, not assumed.** One calibration request fixed chars/token = 4.0774; every
  cell's *actual* length is read back from `usage.prompt_tokens` and reported, never the target.
* **Noise floor** is measured, not assumed: 10 consecutive byte-identical warm sends at 1k and 8k, and
  10 independent fresh-salt cold sends at 1k.

## Result

Median TTFT (ms), n=7 pairs per length. "Resolvable" = |median paired delta| > 2·σ_warm **and** the
bootstrap 95 % CI of the median delta excludes 0.

| L (tok) | miss med (p10–p90) | hit med (p10–p90) | ratio | Δ ms | speed-up | wall miss→hit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1024   | 208.7 (203.4–210.9) | 86.4 (85.7–88.4)   | 0.414 | 122.3  | 2.4×  | 208.8 → 86.5 |
| 2028   | 349.2 (343.4–454.4) | 87.2 (82.6–92.6)   | 0.250 | 262.0  | 4.0×  | 349.3 → 87.3 |
| 3955   | 674.6 (658.9–741.1) | 105.2 (92.5–111.6) | 0.156 | 569.3  | 6.4×  | 674.7 → 105.3 |
| 7863   | 1431.6 (1395.1–1449.6) | 114.3 (111.2–131.4) | 0.080 | 1317.3 | 12.5× | 1431.7 → 114.3 |
| 15701  | 3039.5 (3026.7–3125.1) | 139.2 (132.9–160.7) | 0.046 | 2900.3 | 21.8× | 3039.6 → 139.3 |
| 31415  | 7541.9 (7458.7–7595.2) | 231.6 (226.2–279.5) | 0.031 | 7310.4 | 32.6× | 7542.0 → 231.6 |

Every pair was verified: `cached_tokens == 64*((L-1)//64)` on hits (all 42), `cached_tokens == 0` on
misses (all 42). `wall_ms` tracks TTFT because `max_tokens=1`; it confirms the whole request
shortens, and carries no independent decode information.

**The relationship is not flat.** Power-law fits over the six length medians:

| fit | exponent | r² |
|---|---|---|
| miss TTFT ∝ L^b | **1.052** | 0.993 |
| hit TTFT ∝ L^b | **0.268** | 0.863 |
| ratio (hit/miss) ∝ L^b | **−0.784** | 0.996 |

OLS `TTFT = a + b·L`: miss `b = 0.2407 ms/token` (≈ **4.15 k tok/s** implied prefill throughput),
hit `b = 0.00466 ms/token` (≈ 215 k tok/s) — a **52× smaller length-dependent cost**. The miss curve is
mildly convex (0.204 ms/token at 1k → 0.240 at 32k), so its linear intercept (−279 ms) is a
chord-and-convexity artefact and must **not** be read as negative fixed overhead; the hit intercept
(+79 ms) is the real per-request floor.

**Noise floor.** Warm-path CV = **7.6 %** at 1k and **5.6 %** at 8k; cold-path CV = 8.0 % at 1k.
The box is occasionally bimodal (2 of 10 cold samples ~245 ms vs ~203 ms; 2 of 10 warm ~75 ms vs
~61 ms), so the median is the right statistic. 2σ margins: 9.5 ms at 1k, 15.7 ms at 8k and above.
The smallest effect measured — 122 ms at 1k — is **12.8× the 2σ margin** (the 32k effect is 465×).
Sign test: 7/7 deltas positive at every length, exact p = 0.0078 = the minimum attainable at n=7, so
the effect was resolved at the smallest sample this design allows. Larger n was not needed; a ~5 ms
effect at 1k would have needed n≈700.

**Order does not drive the result.** At 1k, order A gives Δ = 120.2 ms, order B gives 122.1 ms. Across
lengths the medians are 953.9 ms (A) vs 913.9 ms (B) — a 40 ms gap, ≈4 % of the mean delta and within
the spread of a shared box. The direction and sign are identical under both orders.

## Verdict

**The speed claim IS supported.** The hit is faster at all six lengths, the effect grows with L, and
the ratio falls as L^−0.78 — the signature of skipping prefill, not of a flat overhead difference. The
kill condition ("difference inside the noise floor at every length") was not met at any length.
The paper may claim both token economics **and** latency, quantified per length: 122 ms saved at 1k
rising to 7.31 s at 31.4k, i.e. 0.97 of TTFT removed at 32k.

## Findings beyond the brief

1. **U2's cache law needs one refinement: `cached = 64*floor((L-1)/64)`.** U2 tested 44 lengths whose
   residues mod 64 were all **non-zero** (its residue list was 1,2,3,5,…,62 — never 0), where the two
   formulas coincide. At exactly L=1024 the server reuses **960**, not 1024: the last prompt token is
   never reusable. Both the driver and the analyzer now use the refined law; no measured value changed.
2. **An unexplained boundary behaviour at L % 64 == 0.** At exactly 1024 tokens the *immediately*
   repeated request reported `cached_tokens=0` and paid the full cold prefill (207 ms), while a second
   repeat hit 960 and took 86 ms. 3/3 at L=1024, 0/4 at L=1021–1023. Every reported hit was verified,
   so no ratio is contaminated; the mechanism is not established and is named here rather than
   explained away. Worth a dedicated follow-up: it directly affects first-repeat-after-prefill
   workloads.

## Caveats (stated, not hidden)

* Six lengths, n=7 paired reps; chars/token calibrated **once** (at 1k) and extrapolated, so the 32k
  target landed at 31415 tokens and 4k at 3955. Actual lengths are what the table reports.
* The hit's residual slope is **not zero** (0.0047 ms/token): the claim is "≤5 µs/token residual,
  52× below the miss slope", never "zero prefill cost". Part of that residual is the uncached tail
  (L mod 64, 0–64 tokens) plus per-request overhead.
* Cold requests still share the literal `[cell-` prefix with earlier prompts (≤ ~3 tokens); at
  64-token cache granularity this rounds to `cached=0` and is <0.3 % of the shortest prompt.
* One host, one model, one configuration (SGLang hybrid linear attention, `page_size=1`,
  `chunked_prefill_size=8192`); nothing here measures throughput or multi-tenant behaviour.
