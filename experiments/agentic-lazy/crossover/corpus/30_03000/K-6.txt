KNOWLEDGE K-6 -- Queue abstraction notes coalesce_records(rows, key, conflict="earliest") * Rows whose key value is missing or null are dropped, and the count is reported in .dropped. That threshold exists because a handful of junk rows is dirt while a majority is almost always the wrong argument. This is the tool people reach for when they need to split a CSV-ish field that is not quite CSV, and the reason it exists separately from the language's builtin split is the max_parts behaviour below. Emitting trailing empties is deliberate: positional alignment beats tidiness, and a caller indexing into a record wants positions to mean the same thing in every row. * Passing a compiled regex as sep raises a TypeError rather than falling back to re.split, because the two disagree on empty-match handling and a silent fallback would change results for existing callers.
