## Purpose (one)

Settle G9: U10's boundary rule rested on **n = 5 per length** (`1,008 / 1,023 / 1,024 / 1,025 / 1,088`, pooled 35/35 vs 0/15) and never separated "the boundary condition holds" from "why a multiple of 64 misses exactly once". This unit re-measures the same cells at **n = 20** and states precisely what the rule does and does not establish. No other gap was chased.

## Design as executed

Cells and sends per trial (cold → repeat-1 → repeat-2), all with a **fresh fixed-width marker per trial** (`make_salt` → three 4-letter words, constant 5-token cost) so every cold request has a zero-length radix prefix match; each cold row is verified `cached == 0` (85/85 were).

| L | L%64 | trials | sends/trial | requests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 960 | 0 | 5 | 2 | 10 |
| 1023 | 63 | 20 | 2 | 40 |
| 1024 | 0 | 20 | 3 | 60 |
| 1025 | 1 | 20 | 2 | 40 |
| 1088 | 0 | 20 | 3 | 60 |

10 calibration probes (2 per cell) bring the run to exactly **220 requests**. Every trial's prompt was **exactly** the cell length (85/85 cold rows; the tail knob `" the"` closed a 26-token gap after the first probe per cell).

**Why not 9 cells x 20 x 3.** That is 540 live requests against the card's 220 hard cap; even the card's own drop order (4,096 → 2,048 → 1,152 → 960) leaves 5 x 20 x 3 = 300. Resolution (assumption A1): kept the drop-order survivors **plus 960** (a multiple of 64 that G9 names), dropped the redundant non-multiple control **1,008** to pay for it, and gave the two multiple cells three sends each while the two non-multiple controls get two (a repeat-2 on a cell whose repeat-1 always hits tests nothing the boundary cells do not). **Not run: 4,096; 2,048; 1,152; 1,008.** 960 lost two of its seven planned trials to the cap (n=5), and is reported as a spot-check, not a cell estimate.

## What the boundary rule does establish (at these lengths, on this server)

1. **The condition is `L % 64 == 0`, not `(L-1) % 64 == 0`.** 1025 (`L%64 = 1`, the one length where the `(L-1)` rule predicts a miss) is **0/20**; the `(L-1)`-as-miss-rule is refuted outright. 1023 (63) is 0/20 and 1024 is 20/20, so the boundary sits at the multiple itself.
2. **Reproduced in 45/45 at n=20 (CP95 lower 0.9213, no variance observed)**, not established as a property of the boundary: 40/40 immediate repeats at the two boundary cells returned `cached = 0`, with no variance in TTFT (~200/250 ms, full prefill).

> original (superseded): "It is deterministic at n=20, not a 1-in-k race"
3. **"Misses exactly once" is only true at the level of the first repeat, and it understates the cost.** The second repeat hits — but only at `64*floor((L-1)/64) = L - 64` (960 at 1024, 1024 at 1088, **40/40**), never at `L`. So a multiple of 64 never gets a `cached == L` warm request: it pays **one extra full prefill** on the first repeat *and* a **persistent one-block (64-token) tax** on every later repeat measured (rep-3 `L−64` **40/40**, `L` **0/40**; repeats 2–3 of 3, one boundary length pair, mechanism unestablished — `E4R`) — the maximum residue a non-multiple can pay (`L%64 <= 63`), paid every time.

> original (superseded): "a permanent **64-token (one whole block) shortfall** on every later repeat"
4. **(L−1) is the steady-state (rep-2) ceiling at the boundary, not a rep-1 rule; as the miss rule it is refuted in both directions.** The U2 tax law `cached == 64*floor(L/64)` holds 20/20 at 1023 and 20/20 at 1025 but **0/20 at 1024 and 0/20 at 1088**; the `(L-1)` value matches all 40 boundary repeat-2 rows. Both forms agree everywhere else, so only the multiples discriminate them.

> original (superseded): "The (L-1) form is the right ceiling rule at the boundary, and the plain tax law is wrong there"

## What it does not establish

* **The mechanism.** No cause is claimed. Three candidates and their predictions:
  * **C1 one-prefill lag at an exact boundary** — predicts `rep-1 = 0`, `rep-2 = L-64`: matches **every** boundary trial (45/45 zeros, 40/40 at `L-64`).
  * **C2 length accounting `64*floor((L-1)/64)`** — predicts `rep-1 = L-64` at a boundary (960 at 1024), not 0: **contradicted** by the 40/40 zeros, though it does predict the `L-64` ceiling exactly.
  * **C3 a race cleared by a retry/s delay/interleave** — U10's p2/p3 found no effect (delay up to 2 s 20/20 misses; one interleaved 63-token request 5/5 misses); 40/40 immediate misses here give it no support. Not re-tested in this unit.
  These rows separate C2 and C3 from the data, but **C1 vs a still-unknown insertion rule cannot be separated**: `cached_tokens` is the server's own account (G13) and there is no server-side trace of the chunk insertion at the boundary. **The honest state is: condition and cost established, cause not.**
* **Whether the full prefix ever becomes cacheable at a multiple.** A third repeat was not run (no budget); `rep-2 = L-64` is consistent with a permanent one-block ceiling and with a one-more-repeat lag. U10 also stopped at repeat-2.
* **Generality.** 4,096 / 2,048 / 1,152 / 1,008 were not run; 960 is n=5. One model (occamy-1.0, a hybrid-attention NVFP4 checkpoint), one SGLang build, one endpoint — nothing here separates the cache implementation from the model (G4), and no server build hash is in the rows (G13).
* **Prefix-sharing shapes.** Only byte-identical repeats were measured; the U4b fan-out shape (a different suffix after a shared prefix) is not covered.
* Repeat-2 on the non-multiple controls was not run (budget), so "no second miss on non-multiples" rests on the tax-law rows (their repeat-1 already hits).
