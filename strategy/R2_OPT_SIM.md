The model is **one shape everywhere**: `TTFT(turn) = max(0, intercept) + lookup_slope·prompt/1e6
+ (miss_slope + hit_slope)·max(0, uncached − 600)/1000`, with `uncached` derived from the rendered prefix
chain (append layout ⇒ new bytes only; mutable head ⇒ head rewrite; pads ⇒ head break). Every coefficient is
loaded from `constants.json`; `cost_model.py` hard-codes none of them. `calibrate.py` re-derives all of
them; nothing in the constants is hand-typed.

## 3. The model's measured quality (arm C, the no-compaction control, turns 41–80 out of sample)

Fit on turns 1–40 of all four arms (160 rows), graded on arm C's held-out tail:

| Predictor | mean | error vs measured | MAE |
|---|---|---|---|
| measured (oracle level) | **572.0 ms** | — | — |
| `train_mean` (fit-window mean) | 287.5 ms | −49.7 % | 286.0 ms |
| **structure-fixed** (`α + β·turn_cost`, α/β fitted on the fit window) | **306.7 ms** | **−46.4 %** | 265.9 ms |
| `report.json`'s per-arm column | 642.7 ms | +49.5 % | 187.2 ms |
| `regression.py`'s `model` column | 465.6 ms | −18.6 % | 182.4 ms |

**Read this table before trusting any number downstream.**

1. The model grades **worse than the train mean on the held-out window** under the honest out-of-sample
   protocol (MAE 265.9 vs 286.0 ms — it clears the baseline by 7 %, on the wrong side of a 284 ms level shift).
   `test_model_beats_the_train_mean_on_the_holdout` passes only on a **different** predictor (the
   `+bias_ms` form); it does not license "the model predicts the hold-out".
2. The `report.json` column is **not the out-of-sample number its name implies**: its `error_ms` is
   `predicted − measured`, but its `error_pct` is computed against the **all-80-turn** mean, not the 41–80
   mean (−18.6 % and +49.5 % describe the same 642.7 ms prediction). The two are arithmetically inconsistent
   and the table must not be quoted as one.
3. The honest MAE is **182.4 ms against an oracle-mean MAE of 241.7 ms** — the model carries real structure
   (it beats a constant level by 24 %), but on a 40-turn hold-out that contains a level shift it cannot follow.

## 4. The ranking (the SIM card's actual question)

`cost_model.py` on the 9 candidates (`mutable` … `pointer+fold`):
`live-R < live-F < pointer+fold < live-C < pointer+pads < mutable+movable < live-A < append+notes < mutable`
and the two robustness re-runs (`no_full_cost`, `pooled_lookup`) give **the same order**.

`report2.json` — the model rebuilt after SIM's first hold-out failed — gives a different order:
`mutable < live-C < pointer+pads < live-A < append+notes < live-F < live-R < pointer+fold`.

**The two orders disagree at both ends** (`mutable` is first vs last; `live-R`/`live-F` are the top pair vs
the bottom pair), they are **14.3 ms apart on the top pair** (`live-F` 305.7 vs `live-R` 334.4, se 13.5,
i.e. ~2.1 se — below the 3-se threshold the model itself guards with), and **no robustness run was made
inside the agreeing candidate set** — the `no_full_cost`/`pooled_lookup` re-runs that would license a
first-place claim are attached to the SEM model, not to `report2.json`'s.

**`report2.json` is therefore adopted as a set of measured margins, not as a ranking.** Its quotable
contents (each with its own number) are:

- fold vs no-fold on the same workload: `pointer+fold` **334.4** vs `live-C` **285.0** → folding costs
  **+49.3 ms** (se 10.8, ≈4.6 se, `robust: true`);
- `live-F` vs `live-R`: **−28.7 ms** (se 13.5) — direction F < R, **not** licensed as a gap;
- compaction-invariance fits `r² = 0.6309` on the fit window (bias 0.05 ms, sd 61.4 ms);
- its own S4 verdict: the SIM model's arm-C error is **not** a compaction effect (`C` folds nothing), it is
  a **context-cost** effect — a ≥20k prompt adds 0 ms of level and the lookup slope rises ×0.00.

## 5. Defects, named with numbers

### 5.1 `constants.json` is stale: it does not equal a fresh re-derivation (64 fields)

`R2-VLLM` (420 rows, written 21:17 today) is **not** in `constants.json`'s `sources.files` (10 files,
`WIN-SGL` is the newest cell), yet a fresh `calibrate.py` run **does** read it. The differences:

- `small_prompts.pooled`: `n` **210 → 222**, `intercept_ms` **81.28 → 54.86**, slope rse 46.75 → 45.58;
- `behaviour` row count **6,763 → 6,913**; `run_state.rewrite_head_share` **0.8727 → 0.8675**;
- `behaviour.rewrite.turn_increments` **4,177 → 4,293**; `full` **51 → 62**; cell list gains `WIN-VLLM`.

**`live_miss` — the constant the hold-out is judged on — is unchanged** (495.633 µs/tok, n 54, intercept
−20.763 ms), so §3's numbers stand. What is not reproducible is the **behaviour scan and the small-prompt
fit**, i.e. the layout facts the ranking rests on. The card's own rule applies: *every constant carries a
rerun command* → a re-run must reproduce the file, and here it does not.

### 5.2 The `not_coupled` self-check case is real, and mild

`selfcheck.py` scores **red 8/9**, `not_coupled: ["miss slope halved"]`. The suite pins the miss slope on
`turn_cost(5000, 1000, …)` — **an uncached count of 1,000 tokens**. Read from the rows, the largest uncached
count any arm records is **2,280**, C's **41–80** hold-out mean is **719.4**, and the model bills the slope
only on `max(0, uncached − 600)` and only as a **derivative** (`slope + hit_slope(=118.309) = 613.94 µs/tok`),
whose `2001 → 1562` fallback is **6× its own `rse`** (46.75, `small_prompts.pooled`).

**Added guard** (`test_miss_slope_is_coupled_on_measured_rows`): the halved slope must move the **largest
measured** miss by > 100 ms. Measured: **832.66 ms** vs the toy's **198.25 ms** — red under a doubled
constant, green under the real one. The `not_coupled` line in `selfcheck.py` is **kept, not deleted**: it is
a true statement about *the toy turn*, and rewriting the generator's own criteria to make them pass is how a
guard becomes a decoration.

### 5.3 A copy of the model grades a green suite on mutated constants

Discovered while proving the guard above bites. In a **copy** of the model (`scratch/r2-opt-sim/` copied
elsewhere, `tests/` present), with `live_miss.slope_us_per_tok` **doubled** on disk, **22/22 pass** — in a
temp directory *and* in a real copy under `.scratch/simpromote/tmp…`. Yet the same process, run as
`python -c "import test_r2_opt_sim as T"` in the same tree, prints `T.ROOT` pointing **into the copy** and
`T.CONSTANTS['live_miss']['slope_us_per_tok'] = 991.266` — the mutated value. In-tree, the identical
mutation is caught (`1 failed, 21 passed`).

**Consequence:** `selfcheck.py`'s **red 8/9 cannot be reproduced outside the workspace tree** on this
recipe, so its red score is **verified in tree only**. **Not diagnosed** — two mechanisms were tested and
excluded: a mutated `.pyc` (a `cold` mutation in the copy *is* caught) and a `**`-spread dropping a
dunder-like key (the sibling test already documents that trap and avoids it).

### 5.4 The suite pins a superseded artifact

`test_holdout_numbers_are_pinned` and `test_pointer_report_is_degraded_by_design` read
**`report.json`** (134,923 B, `bce60b68…`, schema `resextract/r2-opt-sim/model/1`), not the model that was
adopted. A model change that moves `report2.json` would **not** turn the suite red.

## 8. R2-OPT-SIM's follow-up on §5 — what closed, what did not (appended, nothing above rewritten)

The unit that owns this file re-ran the four open items in SIM-PROMOTE §6. Digests after this pass:
`cost_model.py` `330322f3…`, `selfcheck.py` `37d2aee8…`, `tests/test_r2_opt_sim.py` `ef8a75c7…`,
`calibrate.py` and `constants.json` unchanged (`b1e5e872…`, `bce60b68…`).

**§5.3 — CLOSED, and the diagnosis is not the one §5.3 guessed.** A copy of the model no longer grades a
green suite on mutated constants. The cause was in the pointer protocol, not in caching or bytecode: a
`cell-source` pointer resolved its path through the CALLER's `roots["own"]`, so an out-of-tree copy read
this workspace's `experiments/` and compared the real files against the real files. A `cell-source` is
now pinned to `ROOT / self.path` ("pinned to THIS workspace's bytes regardless of where the process
runs"), and `test_cell_sources_pin_this_workspace` guards it. Two mechanical faults surfaced while
proving it: `selfcheck.py`'s sandbox lives inside `scratch/r2-opt-sim` and copied itself (14 KB of
"File name too long"), and `tests/conftest.py` was not in its copy list.

**§5.2 — CONFIRMED, still not deleted.** `selfcheck.py` scores **red 8/9** in tree, `not_coupled:
['miss slope x2']` (the case is renamed from "miss slope halved" because the generator scales every
case by 2; it wrote 495.633 → 991.266). Six formulations of the miss-slope guard were tried and each
one passed on the mutated file. The reason is the same every time, and it is now recorded in the case:
**a mutation that scales a coefficient is preserved by every comparison that uses that coefficient on
both sides.** Measured instances: a 10x slope moved the turn 3,568.5575999999996 ms against a floor of
9 × the same term = 3,568.5575999999996; a zero-slope anchor missed by 2.8e-14; "zeroed < base" is true
with the slope ignored and with it read. What the guard now checks is a ratio against an independent
constant (`joint_two_term` lookup per 1k prompt tokens vs the miss slope per uncached token), which
holds in all three states — pristine, ×2, ×0.5. It is still **not** a mutation detector, and pretending
otherwise by tuning it until the case flips is how a guard becomes decoration.

**§5.1 — unchanged, and the reason is now a decision.** `constants.json` does not equal a fresh
re-derivation (59–64 fields, mostly `behaviour.rewrite` and `small_prompts.pooled`) because `R2-VLLM`
(420 rows) is on disk and the glob picks it up. `live_miss` — the constant every report number depends
on — is byte-identical either way. **Decision: R2-VLLM stays out.** The file is the record of what the
report was computed from; a constant must cite the cell it came from, and re-deriving it silently would
change the layout facts under a published ranking. Re-issuing the ranking with R2-VLLM in is a new
unit, not a rebuild.

**§5.4 — accepted as a scoping statement, not fixed.** The suite pins `report.json`; `report2.json` is a
different model's output and is adopted only as measured margins (§4). Grading `report2.json` would
mean adopting its arithmetic, which §4 explicitly refuses.

**What a verifier should still do.** Re-run `calibrate.py`, `cost_model.py`, `pytest` and
`selfcheck.py` (all offline, ≈1 min), and check the one ordering this report still calls open: R vs F
(−10.2 ms against a ±5 ms band edge). Everything else in §6 is answered above.
