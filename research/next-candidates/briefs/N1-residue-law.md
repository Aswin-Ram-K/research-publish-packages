# N1 — Alignment, not size: what an append actually costs a prefix cache, and the two-token fix

**Thesis in one sentence.** On a prefix-cached chat server with cache block size *B*, appending *L* tokens to a primed prefix of *P1* tokens re-bills `(P1 mod B) + L` — a quantity set by the history's alignment residue and independent of the payload — and at residue 0 or 1 the cache entry is absent altogether, so an append that should cost 13 tokens costs the whole prefix; padding the history by two tokens before appending converts that cliff into a tail.

**Why it is new.** The in-draft paper's nearest neighbours are **C40/C41** (`papers/context-cache/MANUSCRIPT.md` §9: immediate-repeat misses only at `L ≡ 0 (mod 64)`, 5/5 vs 0/15, Fisher p=3.06e-07; second repeat hits at `64·floor((L−1)/64)`, 10/10), **C55–C57** (§9a: per-stack grid — SGLang 64; vLLM resolved 1,088 / 1,056 / 2,096), and **§8a** (aligning to the grid costs more than it recovers: pad 94.89 mean vs 74.06 recovered). None of them states the append cost law, the vLLM `+B` term, or a rule that fires *before* the append. `papers/context-cache/` mentions the law only in Fold/Compilation plumbing (C76 is the framing-token identity `L_app = n_payload + 12`, not the cost law); grep shows no `+B`, no residue-0 rule, no `(P1 mod B) + L` in `MANUSCRIPT.md`. The contribution is therefore a **closed-form per-append cost law with an exact fit, a discontinuity class, a cross-engine tax ratio, and a client-side intervention** — the paper §8a cannot be, because §8a's frame is "padding usually loses".

**Contradictions a reviewer will find.**
1. **The cliff's magnitude is instrument-dependent, and the record knows it.** `experiments/ptr-engine/RECORD.md` §5 **C1**: a second instrument (`/generate` `meta_info.cached_tokens`) reads residue 0 as re-billing **64 + L**, not the whole prefix, and calls `LAW.md`'s 8,013 a ~**5.2×** overstatement ("0 is a reported zero, NOT a null"); `LAW.md` §3 reads the same event through `usage.prompt_tokens_details` as `cached = null` and bills `P1 + L`. Both instruments agree the **entry is absent** (the identical repeat misses too); they disagree on what a client is charged. The paper must be *about* that: a null in one field and a zero in another are not the same bill, and the padding rule is the only statement that survives under all three readings (2 tokens, 64 tokens, or the whole prefix).
2. **Recovery at `L − 64` is claimed in the manuscript and not reproduced for appends.** `MANUSCRIPT.md` §9 / C40 and `cells/E4`/`E4R` (45/45 vs 0/40, Fisher 3.46e-25; 40/40 at `L − 64` on reps 2–3) measure the *immediate-repeat* shape; `LAW.md` §3 measures a *one-append* shape whose divergence is the 2-token assistant header, and there residue 0 at 7,936 misses exactly like 8,000. Scope the law to the append shape and say so; if the reconciliation cannot be settled from the rows, the settling cell is named and priced, not assumed.
3. **C9's caution is superseded, and the paper must say why.** `RECORD.md` C9 records the earlier avalanche file as HARVESTED (one profile, 378 rows) and `papers/context-cache/FOLD-PROPOSAL.md` bucket (iii) forbids "any residue→avalanche law" because *residue is not separable from P1*. That caution predates the constant-nonce design: `LAW.md` steps `P1` across a whole block at a fixed **10-token** nonce, so `P1 mod B` varies while the prefix stays unique and cold — and the 11/11 positional misses are the separation. **But** the paper must not resurrect the withdrawn claims by accident: `397` as a law of the endpoint, the 5.2×, the 26× "avalanche insurance", and the 141/129 split are all refuted or undecidable and stay out.
4. **§8a points the other way.** "Aligning to the grid costs more than it recovers" is a *page-recovery* argument at a 64-token pad under a particular workload; the residue rule is a *cliff-avoidance* argument at a 2-token pad. Both can be true, but the paper must draw the boundary explicitly or a reviewer will read the two as contradictory.

**Venue and reader value.** MLSys, NSDI, or a serving/systems measurement track; a strong short-paper candidate. Every inference-engine team and every agent-runner author is one padding statement away from the cliff, and the rule is implementable in a request builder in a few lines. The reader value is unusual: a closed-form cost model with an exact residual, a discontinuity worth four orders of magnitude, and an intervention cheaper than the thing it prevents.

**Kill criteria.**
- If a re-measurement shows the residue-0/1 cliff is an artefact of one endpoint build (not reproducible on a second build or a fresh server), the law survives as a tail-cost model and the paper loses its headline — stop and reframe as a note.
- If the two instruments turn out to be reading the *same* counter with different null handling, then C1's 5.2× dispute collapses into a field-semantics footnote and the paper shrinks materially.
- If the `+B` term fails independent re-derivation on vLLM, drop the cross-engine 33× ratio rather than report it second-hand.
- If the paper cannot be written without asserting a mechanism for the cliff, do not write it.

**Abstract draft.** Prefix caching is billed as reuse, but an append re-bills a tail whose size is set by arithmetic nobody instruments. Sweeping the primed length of a SGLang endpoint across a full 64-token block at fixed prompt content, we measure `uncached = (P1 mod 64) + L` with residual exactly zero in 186 of 186 rows, and payload-independence at every residue. At `P1 mod 64 ∈ {0,1}` the behaviour changes class: no cache entry exists, an identical repeat still misses, and an append that adds 13 tokens is charged the whole prefix. The discontinuity is residue-based, not positional — 11 of 11 misses across four block indices — and the same append on vLLM, whose resolved block is 1,056, pays `+B` on top of its tail, 33× the SGLang overhead. We also refute the explanation that such boundaries produce the observed fetch-count sign flip: they account for at most 3.7 % of it. The client-side rule is two tokens: pad the history off residues 0 and 1 before appending.
